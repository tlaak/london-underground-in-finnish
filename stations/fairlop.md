# Fairlop

Fairlop is served by the Central line (Hainault loop), in the London Borough of Redbridge in East London.

## Summary

| London Underground station | Finnish translation |
| -------------------------- | ------------------- |
| Fairlop                    | Isokarsikko         |

## Background

Fairlop is named after the [Fairlop Oak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fairlop_Oak), a celebrated tree in Hainault Forest recorded as the _Fair Lop Tree_ in 1738. It was enormous — a trunk some sixty-six feet round, with seventeen great branches — and "Fair Lop" describes the tree itself: a _fair_ (fine, generous) _lop_, a mighty bough. The tree burned in 1805 and was blown down by 1820.

A huge annual fair did grow up beside it — started by the pump-maker Daniel Day in 1725, drawing hundreds of thousands, and later moving to nearby Barkingside — which makes the market reading tempting. But the fair took its name _from_ the place, not the other way round, and had the name meant a marketplace, Finnish would reach for _turku_ or _tori_ (old words for a market — _turku_ is literally that, and gives the city of Turku its name). The "Fair" in Fairlop is the adjective.

## Direct translations

| English             | Finnish    |
| ------------------- | ---------- |
| fair (fine, great)  | iso, komea |
| lop (a lopped tree) | karsikko   |

The second element is a gift: a [_karsikko_](<https://fi.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karsikko_(mytologia)>) is, in Finnish folk tradition, a tree whose branches are deliberately lopped — a memorial or boundary tree — which matches a "lopped oak" exactly. For "Fair," the sense is "fine" or "great"; given the Fairlop Oak's legendary size, _iso_ ("great, large") fits it best and sounds the most natural — better than the folk-market _Markkinakarsikko_ or the stiffer _Komeakarsikko_.

## Conclusion

Fairlop is "the great lopped oak," not "the fair by the lopped oak." Finnish says it plainly with _iso_ + _karsikko_. Therefore Fairlop station in Finnish shall be called _Isokarsikko_.
