# Angel

Angel is served by the Northern line (Bank branch). It is located in the Angel area of the London Borough of Islington in North London.

## Summary

| London Underground station | Finnish translation |
| -------------------------- | ------------------- |
| Angel                      | Angeli              |

## Background

Angel is [named after the Angel Inn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angel,_London), a coaching inn that stood at the corner of Islington High Street and Pentonville Road and existed from the 17th century. The inn sat on the boundary where the ancient parishes of Clerkenwell and Islington met, and its prominent position at that junction made it the landmark by which the whole locality came to be identified.

It is one of [only five London Underground stations named after a public house](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angel_tube_station) — in this case the once-famous Angel inn, which dates back to at least 1638. Like a great many English inns, "The Angel" took its name and sign from a religious image: the angel, most commonly the archangel Gabriel of the Annunciation.

## Direct translations

| English | Finnish  |
| ------- | -------- |
| angel   | enkeli   |
| inn     | majatalo |

The Finnish word for an angel is _enkeli_, a loanword that reached Finnish through Old Swedish _ängil_ and ultimately from the Greek _ángelos_ ("messenger") — the same Germanic-via-Swedish route that shapes so many of these names. Just as English shortened "The Angel Inn" to simply "Angel," the literal Finnish form would drop _majatalo_ ("inn") and keep only _Enkeli_.

The problem is that _Enkeli_ does not occur as a genuine Finnish place name (much as there is no place called _Pankki_ — see [Bank](bank.md)), so it reads as an invented label rather than a real toponym. The similar-looking Hämeenlinna districts _Engelinranta_ and _Engelinaukio_ are no help either: they are named after the architect [Carl Ludvig _Engel_](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carl_Ludvig_Engel), not after an angel.

A far better fit exists. [Angeli](https://fi.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angeli) is a real village in the municipality of Inari in Finnish Lapland, lying along the Inarijoki river close to the Norwegian border. Its name is of Sámi origin (_Áŋŋel_ in Northern Sámi, _Aŋŋel_ in Inari Sámi) and is not etymologically related to "angel," yet it is all but identical to the English word in sound and spelling and reads completely naturally as a Finnish place name.

## Conclusion

Following the same reasoning as Acton → _Tammela_ — preferring a real, natural-sounding Finnish place name over a stiff literal rendering — the Angel station is best translated not as the literal _Enkeli_ but as _Angeli_, after the Sámi village in Inari. It keeps the sound and feel of the original while standing on genuine Finnish toponymic ground.
