# Snaresbrook

Snaresbrook is served by the Central line (Epping branch), on the boundary of the London Boroughs of Redbridge and Waltham Forest in East London.

## Summary

| London Underground station | Finnish translation |
| -------------------------- | ------------------- |
| Snaresbrook                | Säynepuro           |

## Background

The obvious reading — a brook where _snares_ were set for wildfowl — is folk etymology. [Snaresbrook is in fact a corruption of "Sayer's brook"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Snaresbrook), a tributary of the River Roding that flows through Wanstead. The first element is a person's name, not a trap.

That name is unusually opaque: [_Sayer_ has at least five possible origins](https://surnamedb.com/Surname/sayer) — most likely the Norman personal name _Saher_ (from Germanic _Sigiheri_, "victory-army"), but also a _sawyer_, a professional reciter, an assayer of metals, or a cloth merchant. Which Sayer owned this brook is unknown, so there is no single meaning to carry across.

## Direct translations

| English               | Finnish    |
| --------------------- | ---------- |
| brook                 | puro       |
| Sayer (personal name) | — (a name) |
| snare (folk reading)  | ansa       |

The existing translation _Ansapuro_ ("snare brook") is memorable, but it renders the folk etymology rather than the real one, so it has to go. Since _Sayer_ carries no reliable meaning, the honest approach is the one taken at Chigwell (Cicca → _Kiikka_): keep the "name + brook" shape and reach for a real Finnish element that echoes the sound.

That element is _säyne_: [the ide (_Leuciscus idus_)](https://fi.wikipedia.org/wiki/Säyne), a sizable fish of streams and flowing water. It echoes "Sayer(s)" closely, it is at home in real Finnish place names (Säynätsalo, Säyneinen), and — being a stream fish — it makes _Säynepuro_ read as a wholly natural Finnish brook-name.

## Conclusion

Beneath the snares, Snaresbrook is just somebody's brook, and the name is too worn to recover its owner. _Säynepuro_ keeps the true "name + _puro_" structure and the sound of the original while standing on genuine Finnish ground. Therefore Snaresbrook in Finnish shall be called _Säynepuro_, retiring the folk-etymology _Ansapuro_.
