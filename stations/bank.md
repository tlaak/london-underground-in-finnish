# Bank

Bank station is served by Central, Northern and Waterloo & City lines, and the DLR.

## Summary

| London Underground station | Finnish translation |
| -------------------------- | ------------------- |
| Bank                       | Suomen Pankki       |

## Background

Bank is located in the Bank junction and is named after the [Bank of England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bank_of_England) — not a bank in general, but _the_ Bank, the United Kingdom's central bank. The area, and later the station, took their name from that single landmark institution.

## Direct translations

| English | Finnish |
| ------- | ------- |
| bank    | pankki  |

The direct translation of _bank_ is simply _pankki_ in the original context. Bank could also mean a river bank (_penkka_ in Finnish) but this is not the etymology of the station name.

The bare word _pankki_, however, means merely "a bank" and loses what the English name actually points to: not any bank, but the country's central bank. The faithful Finnish counterpart is the [Bank of Finland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bank_of_Finland), _Suomen Pankki_ — the national central bank founded in 1811, one of the oldest in the world, with its own landmark headquarters in Helsinki. This mirrors the Bank of England exactly: a single, iconic central-bank institution that lends its name to the place around it.

## Conclusion

The plain literal _Pankki_ is workable — I'm not aware of any place called _Pankki_ in Finland, other than a restaurant in Eura, but then "Bank" is no ordinary English place name either. The better test is naturalistic: if a Helsinki metro station stood next to _Suomen Pankki_, it would unquestionably be called _Suomen Pankki_, never merely _Pankki_ — exactly as London named its station after the Bank of England rather than after "a bank." Therefore Bank station in Finnish shall be called _Suomen Pankki_, with the plainer _Pankki_ noted only as the literal one-word rendering.
