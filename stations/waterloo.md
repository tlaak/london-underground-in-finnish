# Waterloo

Waterloo is served by the Bakerloo, Northern, Jubilee and Waterloo & City lines. It sits on the South Bank of the Thames, in the London Borough of Lambeth, beside the great national-rail terminus of the same name.

## Summary

| London Underground station | Finnish translation |
| -------------------------- | ------------------- |
| Waterloo                   | Vesiaho             |

## Background

Waterloo is a name at three removes. The station [is named after Waterloo Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waterloo_station), which was itself named after the [Battle of Waterloo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Waterloo) of 1815 — the bridge opened on the battle's second anniversary. The battle, in turn, took its name from the village of [Waterloo in present-day Belgium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waterloo,_Belgium), first recorded in 1102 at the edge of the Sonian Forest.

The bridge and the battle are purely commemorative names with no descriptive content of their own, so the only layer worth translating is the original Flemish place name. That one is genuinely transparent.

## Direct translations

| English                                         | Finnish |
| ----------------------------------------------- | ------- |
| water                                           | vesi    |
| _loo_ (clearing in a forest; cf. _lea_/_leigh_) | aho     |

The first element is simply _water_ → _vesi_. The second, [_loo_](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/loo) (Dutch, from Proto-Germanic _\*lauhaz_), means "a clearing in a forest," or "a forest on sandy soil with many clearings." It is the exact cognate of the Old English _lēah_ that gives English its _-ley_ and _-leigh_ names. The precise Finnish counterpart is [_aho_](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/aho): a glade, a grassy clearing in a (former) forest. So _Waterloo_ becomes _Vesiaho_, "water-clearing" — very nearly word-for-word.

The tempting alternative _Vesikangas_ (with _kangas_, a dry sandy heath) catches the "sandy soil" side of _\*lauhaz_ but misses the defining clearing — and worse, _kangas_ is by definition dry, so pairing it with _vesi_ revives exactly the wet/dry contradiction settled at [Morden](morden.md), where _suo_ was chosen over the dry-heath _nummi_. _Aho_ has no such problem. It is also a real, if uncommon, element of Finnish nature-surnames, so _Vesiaho_ stands on genuine ground.

## Conclusion

Beneath the bridge and the battlefield, Waterloo is plain Flemish for "the clearing by the water." Finnish says the same thing with _vesi_ + _aho_. Therefore Waterloo in Finnish shall be called _Vesiaho_.
