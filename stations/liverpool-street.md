# Liverpool Street

Liverpool Street is served by the Central, Circle, Hammersmith & City and Metropolitan lines, in the north-eastern corner of the City of London.

## Summary

| London Underground station | Finnish translation |
| -------------------------- | ------------------- |
| Liverpool Street           | Mutalahdenkatu      |

## Background

Like [Waterloo](waterloo.md), this is a name at several removes. The station is named after the street, which was [renamed in 1829 (from _Old Bethlem_) after Lord Liverpool](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liverpool_Street_station), the Prime Minister Robert Jenkinson, 2nd Earl of Liverpool. His earldom in turn takes its name from the city of Liverpool.

Only that last layer carries any descriptive meaning, and it is a fitting one: [Liverpool derives from Old English _lifer_, "thick or muddy water," and _pōl_, "a pool or creek"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liverpool) — the muddy tidal creek at the city's origin.

## Direct translations

| English               | Finnish |
| --------------------- | ------- |
| _lifer_ (muddy water) | muta    |
| _pōl_ (pool, creek)   | lahti   |
| street                | katu    |

_Lifer_ (muddy water) gives _muta_ (mud), and _pōl_ — a tidal pool or creek — is best rendered _lahti_, an inlet or bay. With _katu_ for street, the whole becomes _Mutalahdenkatu_, "muddy-creek street." This is also the professional answer to the name the project's README singles out as a temptation for a joke.

## Conclusion

Beneath the Prime Minister and his earldom, Liverpool is a muddy creek. Liverpool Street station in Finnish shall therefore be called _Mutalahdenkatu_.
