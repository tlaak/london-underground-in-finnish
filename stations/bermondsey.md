# Bermondsey

Bermondsey is served by the Jubilee line, in the London Borough of Southwark, south-east of the City. It was once low, marshy ground by the Thames, and the name records a drier patch of firm land within it.

| Bermondsey                 | Karhuvartiansaari   |

## Background

Bermondsey [means "_Beornmund_'s island"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bermondsey): the Old English personal name _Beornmund_ followed by _eg_, "island," or more precisely "a piece of firm land in a fen." It need not have been a true island — as likely a higher, drier spot in an otherwise marshy area. The earliest forms are _Vermundesei_ (a privilege said to have been granted around 708–715, surviving in a 12th-century copy) and _Bermundesy_ / _Bermundesye_ in the Domesday Book of 1086.

The _-ey_ ending places Bermondsey in the family of Thames "islands" — Battersea, Chelsea, Hackney — dry rises in the marsh. Finnish names its own marsh-and-island districts exactly the same way, as in Helsinki's [_Lauttasaari_](https://fi.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lauttasaari), _Kulosaari_ and _Korkeasaari_.

## Direct translations

| English                       | Finnish         |
| ----------------------------- | --------------- |
| _beorn_ (warrior, man; bear)  | karhu           |
| _mund_ (protection, guardian) | vartija, vartia |
| _eg_, _-ey_ (island)          | saari           |

_Beornmund_ is a two-element Germanic name of the usual heroic type: _beorn_ + _mund_. In Old English usage [_beorn_](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/beorn) means "warrior" or "man," but it is a phonetically exact — if scholarly disputed — cognate of Old Norse _bjǫrn_, "bear" — Finnish _karhu_ — which gives a vivid and real Finnish handle on the element. _Mund_ means "protection" or "guardianship," the protecting hand; the natural Finnish agent for it is a guardian or keeper — _vartija_ in modern spelling, or the older, poetic _vartia_. (The related _vartio_ means the watch or guard-duty rather than the person, so it fits less well here — though it shows the guard family is at home in real Finnish island names, as in Helsinki's [_Vartiosaari_](https://fi.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vartiosaari).) Since _Beornmund_ is an ancient name, the archaic _vartia_ suits it better than the modern _vartija_.

Read together, _beorn_ + _mund_ reconstructs as the byname _Karhuvartia_, "bear-guardian" — a plausible warrior's epithet in a suitably old-fashioned form. The _-ey_ then attaches as _saari_.

## Conclusion

Bermondsey is "_Beornmund_'s island," and the Finnish keeps that exact shape: the personal name in the possessive, followed by the island. _Beornmund_ becomes _Karhuvartia_ ("bear-guardian," from _beorn_/bear + _mund_/guardian, in the archaic _vartia_ form that suits an old name), and _Beornmund's island_ becomes _Karhuvartian saari_, compounded to one word: _Karhuvartiansaari_.

A leaner _Karhusaari_ ("Bear Island") would have been a ready-made name — Finnish already uses it, e.g. as the name of Norway's arctic [_Karhusaari_ (Bjørnøya)](https://fi.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karhusaari), whose Norwegian original _björn_ is the very cognate behind our _karhu_ — but it drops the _-mund_ half of the name. Since the whole difficulty of Bermondsey is that it commemorates one particular person, it is worth translating that person's name in full. Therefore Bermondsey in Finnish shall be called _Karhuvartiansaari_, with _Karhusaari_ noted as the shorter alternative.
